Frequently Asked Questions

What does CitationSniper actually do?

You select a sentence or claim on a webpage or PDF, and CitationSniper searches reputable academic databases for research related to it. Each result is analysed and labelled as supporting, contradictory, mixed, or related, so you can tell at a glance whether a source is worth citing.

Is it free?

Yes. The free plan gives you a set number of citation searches per day with results from every connected database. A Pro plan lifts the daily cap and adds more results per search, full abstracts, extra citation styles, and a saved citation library.

Can it really detect fake or AI-generated citations?

It checks whether each reference resolves to a real record in the databases it searches. If a reference cannot be found, it is flagged so you can investigate. A source that cannot be verified is not proof it is fake — it may simply sit outside the databases we search — so treat flags as a prompt to check, not a verdict.

Which databases does it search?

Reputable academic sources including Semantic Scholar, CrossRef, OpenAlex, Europe PMC, and CORE, queried at the same time rather than one after another.

Does it work on PDFs?

Yes. You can select text directly in a PDF opened in your browser, the same way you would on a web page.

Which citation styles can I export?

APA is available on the free plan. Pro adds MLA, Chicago, and Harvard. You can copy a formatted reference or export straight to Word, Google Docs, or plain text.

What do you do with the text I select?

Selected text is sent to our service to run the search and analysis, and is not used to build a profile of you. CitationSniper does not read pages in the background or track your general browsing. Full detail is in the Privacy Policy.

Should I trust the AI analysis on its own?

No. Confidence scores and evidence labels are there to help you shortlist quickly, but AI analysis can be wrong. Always read the source before you cite it — the tool is there to speed up your judgement, not replace it.
